<details><summary><code>a02</code> How does SHARPO assign credit in agentic reinforcement learning?</summary>

**Python**

> SHARPO addresses the limitation of trajectory-level rewards in agentic reinforcement learning, which often fail to distinguish helpful intermediate steps from errors that lead to task failure [2610.00838v1]. Instead of assigning a single advantage to all tokens in a rollout, SHARPO implements segment-level credit assignment by refining Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). It divides the interaction trajectory into environment-facing segments and leverages on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) principles [2610.00838v1].
>
> Specifically, the method uses a copy of the agent's policy as a teacher to evaluate the student's behavior. SHARPO computes log-probability gaps between the teacher and student within each segment, using this signal to generate a bounded multiplier for the GRPO advantage [2610.00838v1]. This multiplier is then shared by all tokens inside that specific segment, allowing credit to vary across different segments of the interaction while avoiding token-level granularity [1]. By aggregating these teacher-student feedback signals from successful rollouts within a group, SHARPO provides dense supervision that adjusts the magnitude of outcome-derived advantages based on local performance rather than just the final result [2610.00838v1]. This approach ensures that actions contributing to success receive appropriate credit even if they occur early in a long multi-step sequence.

**PaperPilot**

> SHARPO assigns credit by refining Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) at the level of environment-facing segments rather than applying a single advantage to all tokens in a trajectory. In standard GRPO, a sparse reward or negative advantage derived from the final outcome is shared across every token generated, meaning both useful steps and errors receive identical feedback. SHARPO addresses this by leveraging on-policy self-distillation (OPSD). It computes teacher-student log-probability gaps within each semantic segment using a successful rollout from the same group as a "teacher" [arXiv:2610.00838v1]. These gaps provide fine-grained signals about how much the teacher supports the student's behavior relative to a successful completion of the task. The method aggregates these gaps and applies them to compute a bounded multiplier on the original GRPO advantage. This multiplier is then shared by all tokens within that specific segment, allowing credit attribution to vary across different parts of the interaction [arXiv:2610.00838v1]. Consequently, this approach distinguishes actions that contributed to success from those that led to failure, improving performance on benchmarks like ALFWorld and WebShop compared to baselines such as GRPO, SDAR, and RLSD [arXiv:2610.00838v1].
